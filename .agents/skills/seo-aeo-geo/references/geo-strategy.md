# GEO Strategy

Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) is the practice of building the
conditions under which generative AI systems — including LLM-based chat
assistants, AI overviews, and AI-powered search — can accurately and
confidently reference your brand, product, or organization.

GEO is **not** "AI SEO." It is not a set of technical tricks. It is the
long-term work of establishing a brand as a legitimate, well-documented,
independently verifiable entity.

---

## Core Principle

There are two fundamentally different approaches to building authority:

### Claimed authority

```text
"Our platform is the leading solution for waste management in Southeast Asia."
"Trusted by thousands of municipalities worldwide."
"Award-winning technology."
```

These statements are:
- Unverifiable without a source
- Indistinguishable from marketing copy
- Ignored or discounted by AI systems evaluating trustworthiness
- Potentially penalized if provably false

### Earned authority

```text
"eHakot is currently used by the Municipality of Cebu for daily waste
collection scheduling across 12 barangays."
"The system integrates with the Department of Environment's waste
reporting standards."
"Implementation guidance is publicly available at [URL]."
```

These statements are:
- Specific and verifiable
- Corroborated by third-party sources where possible
- Attributable to real entities
- Useful to AI systems building a knowledge graph

**GEO is the process of producing the second type of authority, not the first.**

---

## Why GEO Matters for Generative AI

When a generative AI system generates text about a brand or product, it draws
on patterns from its training data. The quality and volume of that training
signal depends on:

1. **How often the brand is mentioned across the web** — in documentation,
   articles, directories, forums, integrations, and third-party references
2. **Whether descriptions are consistent** — the same entity described in
   the same way, with the same name, across different sources
3. **Whether claims are corroborated** — a product feature described in
   your documentation, then referenced by an integration partner, then
   mentioned in a forum discussion carries more weight than a claim on
   your own marketing page alone
4. **Whether the content is expert-quality** — thin, vague, or duplicated
   content does not build GEO signal

## GEO Pillars

### 1. Brand and Entity Consistency

Every reference to your brand, product, or service across the web should use:
- The same official name (not variations or acronyms unless you own them)
- The same category description
- The same primary use case description

**Audit:** Search for your brand name. Do the top results describe your product
consistently? Are there conflicting descriptions? Are old descriptions that no
longer apply still indexed?

**Action:** Publish a canonical "About" or "What is [Product]?" page that
clearly defines the brand, product, and category. Keep it factual and current.

---

### 2. Public Documentation

Documentation is one of the highest-value GEO assets because:
- It is specific and verifiable
- It demonstrates real product capability
- It can be indexed and referenced by third parties
- It provides AI systems with structured, factual information about how your product works

**Minimum documentation to create:**
- Product overview (what it is, who it is for, what it does)
- Installation or setup guide
- Core feature documentation
- API documentation (if applicable)
- Configuration reference

Documentation quality is GEO signal. Thin or incomplete documentation
reduces the AI signal for your product.

---

### 3. Case Studies and Evidence

A case study is a **verifiable account of how a real user or organization
used your product and what resulted**.

Case study quality criteria:
- Names the customer (if consent given), or describes them with enough
  specificity to be credible (e.g., "a mid-sized municipality in Cebu Province")
- Describes the specific problem the customer had
- Describes what eHakot did to address it
- Describes the outcome with specifics (e.g., "reduced scheduling errors by
  approximately 40% in the first quarter" — if this is a real measured result)

**Do not invent case studies, statistics, or customer names.**
A fabricated case study is worse than no case study, both ethically and for
long-term trust.

---

### 4. Third-Party References

Third-party references are mentions of your brand on domains you do not control.

High-value third-party references include:
- Government or institutional integrations that publish documentation
- Industry directories and listings
- Partner integrations and case studies on partner sites
- Forum discussions by real users
- Academic or research papers that cite your product
- News coverage (factual, not press releases republished as news)
- Technical blog posts by practitioners who use your product

**How to earn third-party references:**
- Build integrations with platforms that maintain public directories
- Contribute to open standards or public sector procurement processes
- Publish research or analysis that others cite
- Create genuinely useful tools or data that practitioners link to
- Make your product available for evaluation or academic use

**Do not pay for fake links, reviews, or citations. This is prohibited.**

---

### 5. Expert Content

Expert content demonstrates that the team behind the product understands the
domain at a professional level.

Examples:
- A technical blog post on waste management data standards
- A guide on how municipalities can evaluate waste management software
- A whitepaper on collection route optimization
- A comparison of GPS tracking approaches for field operations
- Contributions to public sector technology discussions

Expert content:
- Attracts links from practitioners in the field
- Establishes the brand as a domain authority (not just a vendor)
- Provides AI systems with evidence that the brand has expertise in its domain
- Can be cited in academic or institutional contexts

---

### 6. Author and Organizational Credibility

AI systems increasingly weight content by the credibility of its authors and
the organization behind it.

**Actions:**
- Create public author profiles with real names and verifiable credentials
- Link author profiles to external professional profiles (LinkedIn, GitHub,
  institutional pages) where appropriate
- Make organizational information transparent: who is behind the product,
  where the organization operates, how to contact them
- Use `Person` and `Organization` structured data where appropriate

---

### 7. Consistency Across Time

A brand that has been consistently described in the same way for several years
carries more GEO weight than a brand that reinvents its description annually.

**Do not:**
- Change the product name without a deliberate migration strategy
- Abandon old documentation URLs without redirects
- Delete historical content that has earned references

---

## What GEO Is Not

| Not this | Why |
|----------|-----|
| Buying AI-ready backlink packages | Fabricated links, valueless at best, penalized at worst |
| Writing pages designed to appear in AI answers | Content should be genuinely useful, not reverse-engineered from AI output formats |
| Fabricating user reviews or testimonials | Unethical, verifiably false, and counterproductive |
| Keyword-stuffing content with AI-sounding phrases | AI systems detect low-quality content patterns |
| Creating hundreds of thin, auto-generated pages | Thin content is a GEO negative signal |
| Claiming false partnerships or clients | A single verifiable falsehood undermines credibility |

---

## GEO Audit Questions

Ask these for the application you are working on:

1. Is there a clear, factual, publicly accessible description of what the product is?
2. Is public documentation comprehensive enough to demonstrate real product capability?
3. Are any case studies or evidence pages published? Are they factual and specific?
4. Is the brand described consistently across all pages of the application?
5. Are there third-party references to the product on domains you do not control?
6. Is there any expert content that demonstrates domain expertise?
7. Are author and organizational details transparent and verifiable?
8. Are all claims on the site verifiable or appropriately qualified?

---

## GEO Priority Model

| Level | Action |
|-------|--------|
| P1 | Ensure product description is factual, clear, and publicly indexed |
| P1 | Publish real documentation for the product |
| P2 | Create at least one genuine case study or evidence page |
| P2 | Ensure brand name and description are consistent across all pages |
| P3 | Publish expert content on topics related to the domain |
| P3 | Earn third-party references through integrations, contributions, or research |

---

## Relationship Between GEO and SEO/AEO

GEO does not replace SEO or AEO. It builds on them:

```text
Without SEO: AI crawlers may not discover or trust the content
Without AEO: AI systems cannot extract accurate information from the content
Without GEO: AI systems lack corroborating evidence to confidently cite the brand
```

A technically sound, content-clear website with no external evidence will
still have limited GEO signal. A well-documented brand with rich external
references but broken SEO will lose much of that GEO value at the discovery layer.

Invest in all three layers, in order.
